# Recipient safety repair

## Outbound hold (lifted 2026-11-05 by the owner)

The unconditional hold was lifted by the owner after the recipient-verification gate passed production checks: Verifalia authenticated, and 5 queued inboxes were verified Deliverable within 24 h. Sending now follows the dashboard's outreach mode, still subject to every rule below.

**Emergency brake:** set repository variable `OUTREACH_HOLD=YES` (GitHub → Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions → Variables). The next cycle forces `OUTREACH_MODE=off`, and research, reply reading and backlink checks keep running. The dashboard's ■ Stop button pauses the whole schedule. Previously accepted messages may still be retried by Gmail; the brake only controls new Autopilot sends.

## Recipient eligibility

The built-in Gmail sender requires all of the following before opening SMTP:

1. The address is literally published on a current first-party publisher page. It is never constructed from a name, role or guessed address pattern.
2. The published context identifies an editorial route or matching author. Rank a named author first, an editorial inbox next, an explicitly designated generic editorial route after that. **Owner decision (Nov 2026):** as a last resort, when none of those exist, a general inbox (`hello`, `hi`, `contact`, `info`, `team`, `mail`, `office`, `enquiries`) on the publisher's own domain that is published on its own contact or about page (including the indie-blog names `/contact-me`, `/about-me`, `/work-with-me`, `/get-in-touch`) may be used (rank 60), still subject to every rule below (mailbox check must be Deliverable; catch-all never passes). Do not use support, sales, privacy, feedback, free-webmail or unrelated third-party addresses.
3. The destination domain passes the DNS check. Null MX, unresolved DNS and absent explicit MX are held, not treated as mailbox existence. Some domains can receive mail without explicit MX; the conservative policy deliberately holds them.
4. A real mailbox verifier gives a complete positive verdict for that exact email, checked within 24 hours. Catch-all, unknown, pending, blocked, disposable, malformed and error responses never authorize a send.
5. The address and publisher are not suppressed, and the reply scan completed successfully with a current cursor. A backlog is a reason to hold, not to ignore old opt-outs.
6. The recipient is checked again for every follow-up. Do not silently change contacts in an existing conversation.
7. Gmail's Sent folder is read (read-only, To/Date/Message-ID headers only) every cycle before anything is sent, covering the whole 90-day contact window (paged back past the newest 1000 messages). An address found there is treated as already contacted even if the state file has no record of it, and a recipient who received two emails within 24 hours gets no further email. The same address is never pitched twice within 90 days, even when it is published on two different sites: the check runs after verification too, since verification may switch to another published address. If the Sent folder cannot be read in full, nothing is sent that cycle (and no automatic answer goes out).
8. A contact, about, team or write-for-us page that asks not to be pitched anywhere on it ("we don't accept unsolicited pitches", "please don't pitch us", "no link requests", "unsolicited pitches will be ignored") gives no address at all.

The `RecipientVerifier` uses bounded first-party page fetches and at most three verified contact candidates per prospect. It does not scrape a third-party contact directory or probe mailbox permutations.

## Owner decisions (Nov 2026): 3-way exchanges, the closing sentence, the header, Saturday mornings, contact forms

These replace the earlier copy rules that banned any exchange offer. Nothing above is loosened: every email still needs a first-party published address with a positive mailbox check under 24 hours (catch-all and unknown never pass), suppressions and opt-outs are always honoured, a publisher domain is contacted at most once per 90 days, and no paid verification credit is spent without the opt-in.

**3-way (ABC) link exchange** (`LINK_EXCHANGE`, default `abc`; `off` asks for the link only):

- Publisher B is asked to link to our site A. In return the owner places a contextual link to one of B's articles from a genuinely related page on one of the owner's *other* sites, C. C is never A (no A↔B swaps) and is always one of the sites in `functions/_lib/sites.js`. Every email and answer that mentions C says plainly that it is "a separate site I run"; it is never presented as independent, a partner's or someone else's site. Answers check this again before writing an offer: an offer whose C is A (or shares its domain) or is not one of the owner's sites is never written, and the owner is flagged instead.
- No payment is ever offered or accepted automatically. A fee, a guest post, or a request for the link back to come from A itself ("the same site", "directly", "your site", A's domain) or from a site we did not offer needs the owner (`needs_owner`): a fee gets no answer, the others a questions-only draft, and none is ever sent automatically. Any doubt lands with the owner.
- Caps: at most `EXCHANGE_PER_SITE_MONTH` (default 6) offers per partner site per month and 2 per partner page. New offers pause while 3 or more return links are overdue, until the owner catches up. An offer drafted as an answer holds its page for as long as the owner may still send it (120 days); an older draft is picked again before it is reused. An offer that went out onto a page that filled up meanwhile (a late draft, or a lapsed offer their link revives) is kept, since it was promised, and flagged so the owner may place the link on another related page of the same site instead; Autopilot then finds it there and records that page.
- One exchange runs per publisher domain at a time, across all of its prospects (one publisher can be known to several of our sites), and C is never a site that publisher already links to, nor one that already gives it a return link: no C↔B loop and no A↔B swap through an earlier exchange. A partner page is "genuinely related" only when it shares at least 3 specific words with their article, one of them from the partner page's own title.
- Autopilot has no write access to the sites: the owner places the return link. The obligation is recorded on the prospect (offered → accepted → their link live → owed within 7 days → fulfilled, or declined/lapsed) and both links are verified from crawls. No email claims a link is already in place, invents traffic or authority figures for our sites, or promises anything Autopilot cannot track (no dates, no link attributes, no further links).
- A reply that says yes or names the link back we offered ("sure, if you link back", "deal", "happy to swap") accepts the offer (a neutral reply that only mentions a swap never does: it gets the recap draft): the exchange becomes accepted and the conversation positive, with no owner flag, since the owner approved these exchanges in advance. The answer confirms the exact deal in plain words: their article, our page and its link text, and the page on C that will link to their article as soon as theirs is live (or "now that yours is live" when their reply says it is up). With `REPLY_MODE=send` that confirmation may go out automatically under every gate listed under "Replies and recovery", and only for a plain yes or one that names C, adding no terms of its own (a link attribute, a placement, a deadline, who goes first); the only difference is that the link back we offered ourselves no longer counts as a condition. A yes that says where the link back should come from without naming C ("from your shoe guide", "from Gear Up To Fit", "the running one, not the plant one", "the site I'm linking to"), or that attaches "but", "prefer", "only", "must" or "as long as" to it, is a draft for the owner; one that wants it from anywhere but C is a counter (`needs_owner`), and the offer as made then starts no obligation when they link (it lapses as countered) unless they later accept it as made. A question about the link back gets a draft that recaps the offer. An opt-out declines an exchange they have not linked for yet; one whose link is already live is still owed.
- A link-back request on an older pitch that offered no exchange gets a draft offering the 3-way exchange when a partner page is free (else the clarifying questions). That offer is on record only once the owner sends the draft (its id in Gmail's Sent folder, or their reply to it).
- Which publisher and which page on C live only in the encrypted vault and the dashboard's unlocked view (Prospects tab, "Exchanges": their article, our page that links back, the page they link to, and the due date). Public files, logs and issues carry counts only.
- Every cycle the `exchanges` stage runs after the link checks: opt-outs decline open offers, a bounce lapses one, offers with no answer lapse after 60 days (120 once answered), and each owed return link is looked for on its page on C (crawl first, then at most 10 plain fetches of our own pages), whatever host their article is on (wordpress.com and share- paths included). Two obligations to one publisher on one page each need a link to their own article. A publisher page that answers 404 or 410 twice, at least 7 days apart, is gone, and so is their link: a return link not yet placed is no longer owed. A follow-up restates the first email's offer only while C is still one of the owner's sites (one no longer listed ends the offer) and is not failing. Return links coming due raise `exchange_owed` as a warning (an issue); once one is overdue the same issue escalates to an action, with both counts in it.
- Publishers in an exchange are left out of the public list of won links, and the link-check result and log carry no per-cycle exchange count, so the public file cannot name an exchange partner.

**Closing sentence instead of the P.S.:** the P.S. 'reply "no"' line is gone (a model-written P.S. is removed, and one that is left sends the template instead). Every first pitch and follow-up ends with one short, natural sentence that still says how to stop all email (for example "If it's not a fit, just say so and I won't follow up."). That opt-out explanation is never removed. Replies such as "no", "not a fit", "isn't a fit for us", "not for us", "not relevant", "not suitable", "not for me", "no interest", "please don't follow up", "no need to follow up", "thanks, but no", "no deal", "not now" or "please stop" are opt-outs, also from a no-reply or help-desk address; one that also says yes ("not a fit for the intro, so I added it lower down") is left to the owner, never suppressed; our own sentence quoted back ("if it's not a fit…") is not an opt-out.

**What a model-written pitch may say:** the AI writer's email is checked before it can be sent, and the template is used instead when it names any site other than ours, the publisher's own, C (with an offer) and the sites in its facts; when it offers anything in return without an offer, or offers a second favour (from A, "the page above", our tool, a site we do not run); when anything in it claims a link is already in place (except our recorded citation of their article), promises traffic or a permanent, site-wide or dofollow link, offers money in any wording, or calls C independent or a partner's site; or when the sentence naming C does not say it is "a separate site I run".

**List-Unsubscribe header:** off by default, because it marks one-to-one mail as bulk (`LIST_UNSUBSCRIBE_HEADER=on` adds it to pitches, follow-ups and answers). The plain-words opt-out stays in every pitch and follow-up, and an "unsubscribe" subject or one of our ids in a subject is still an opt-out.

**Saturday mornings** (09:00–13:00 in the recipient's own time zone, daylight saving included) are a send window (`SATURDAY_SENDS`, default on; `NO` keeps weekdays only). A domain that spans zones sends only in the hours that are Saturday morning in all of them (`.com` on both US coasts: 17:00–18:00 UTC in winter; `.au` in Sydney and Perth). Pre-verification, weekend screening, the waker and the outcome watchdog (a Saturday with open windows counts as a send day) follow the same windows; the 24-hour proof rule is unchanged.

**Contact-form queue:** a publisher that publishes only a contact form gets a prepared message for the owner to paste by hand, at most `FORM_QUEUE_DAILY` (default 5) a day. Forms are never submitted automatically. Preparing the message counts as contacting that publisher domain, so it is never also emailed within 90 days. A reply from an address at that publisher's domain, for 150 days after the message whatever has happened since, or from a webmail address that quotes our message (our page's URL with their domain or title), is matched to it when nothing else matches, and is handled like any other reply (an opt-out suppresses the address and the publisher's domain). The address they answered from is kept, so their later messages and answers to our answers match too. A form system's acknowledgement (wordpress@, noreply@, "we have received your message") is an automatic reply and never takes that match. The dashboard's unlocked Prospects tab shows each message with the form link and the text to paste; a message with no reply expires after 21 days (the domain stays contacted), and the publisher's page is checked for a link like an emailed one.

## Provider configuration (not enabled by this repair)

### Verifalia (primary, free) and Hunter (fallback)

| Type | Name | Requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Secret | `VERIFALIA_USERNAME` / `VERIFALIA_PASSWORD` | A Verifalia user (preferably a dedicated sub-user). HTTP Basic auth header; never in URLs. |
| Variable | `VERIFALIA_QUALITY` | `Standard` (1 credit, default), `High` (2) or `Extreme` (4). |
| Variable | `VERIFALIA_ALLOW_PAID` | `YES` lets checks use purchased credit packs. Default: free daily credits only, read from `/credits/balance` before any submission, so verification cannot cost money. |
| Setting | `HUNTER_DAILY_LIMIT` | Hunter's own daily check budget (maximum 50). Default `0`: Hunter is never called, even with a key saved. |
| Setting | `OUTREACH_DACH` | `YES` only after the owner has checked the rules for Germany, Austria and Switzerland. Default: held (see below). |

A send needs `classification=Deliverable`, `status=Success` and no disposable flag, for the exact address. `ServerIsCatchAll`, `Risky` and `Unknown` hold; `Undeliverable` suppresses the address. Each finished job is deleted from Verifalia right after it is read. A submission is never re-sent after an uncertain network error. Credit savers: catch-all domains are remembered for 90 days and skipped (no provider can prove an inbox there), inconclusive answers are not re-bought for 3 days, and checks run at send time plus, each cycle, on that day's leftover free credits for the highest-ranked queued inboxes whose send window opens within 20 hours (so every proof is still under 24 hours old when the email goes out; nothing is checked on Friday night for Monday; Saturday-morning windows count like weekday ones). Hunter is called only within its own `HUNTER_DAILY_LIMIT` budget and never shares Verifalia's counter: as a second opinion when Verifalia cannot decide (Unknown, not catch-all, not Risky), and as the fallback when Verifalia has no free credits left that day or is unavailable; only `valid` with `accept_all=false` passes. The order in which queued inboxes are verified follows the learned pass rate of their mail host (from MX records, a free DNS lookup) multiplied by link likelihood; this changes only which inbox is checked first, never what counts as a pass. An inbox with a cached inconclusive answer is skipped until that answer can change, and repeated "unknown" answers wait 3, 6, 12, then 24 days. Each day spends at most min(`EMAIL_VERIFY_DAILY_LIMIT`, Verifalia's live free credits). Pre-verification runs only while outreach is on (never with `OUTREACH_MODE=off` or `OUTREACH_HOLD=YES`), checks due follow-ups before new prospects, keeps credits for follow-ups due later that day, skips addresses already emailed, and stops once fresh proofs cover the day's remaining sending capacity, counting each page's limit of 2 first emails a day. After a server-level "unknown" (SMTP or DNS timeout, server unavailable, greylisting) no more credits go to that mail domain in the same cycle. A catch-all answer retires the prospect at once.

Saturday mornings (09:00–13:00 in the recipient's time) are send windows, so Friday's and Saturday's credits back Saturday sends under the same 24-hour rule; Sunday never sends, and its credits are kept for Monday's windows. When no queued inbox's send window comes within 20 h before the day's credits reset (Saturday afternoon and evening once the last Saturday window has closed; Friday night and all of Saturday when `SATURDAY_SENDS=NO`), leftover Verifalia credits screen the best queued inboxes. Screening only ever uses free credits that would otherwise expire, even with `VERIFALIA_ALLOW_PAID=YES`. A screen answer is stored as screen-only and never authorizes a send, however recent: "invalid" and catch-all retire the inbox, and "valid" only moves it up the queue for its next window, where a fresh check is still required. Hunter is never used for screening. A per-day ledger of checks, credits, outcomes and emails sent (counts only) is published with the verifier status.

**DE/AT/CH (owner opt-in):** unless `OUTREACH_DACH=YES`, an address is held before any page fetch or credit when the publisher domain or the address domain ends in `.de`, `.at` or `.ch`, or when the address comes from an Impressum, imprint or Kontakt page (`jurisdiction_consent_required`; the prospect retires after two such holds).

The dashboard (⚙ Connect) can store the key as the `HUNTER_API_KEY` secret and the daily limit in the encrypted `LINKFORGE_SETTINGS` secret. A limit above 0 records the owner's opt-in (`EMAIL_VERIFICATION_ENABLED=YES`), and 0 turns it off. Dashboard settings take precedence over repository variables. Without a usable verifier the outreach stage is skipped and reports why: no page fetches, no provider calls, no mail. This does not lift the workflow's outbound hold.

The adapter uses the official `https://api.hunter.io/v2/email-verifier` endpoint with `X-API-KEY`. API keys do not go into URLs or logs. A provider response is not an inbox-placement guarantee. Provider costs are separate from DataForSEO costs; no paid calls are made without the opt-in and budget. Attempts are counted even on timeout; uncertain requests are not automatically retried. Usage is stored with the encrypted state at the normal cycle checkpoint, so the counter is not a crash-proof transactional billing ledger.

## Replies and recovery

The reader uses Gmail's All Mail special-use mailbox when exposed, falling back to INBOX. It processes bounded oldest-first batches with a mailbox/UIDVALIDITY-scoped cursor; it excludes the sender's own messages. Incomplete scans hold new sends until caught up. It reads a bounded body and handles common plain-text, HTML, base64, quoted-printable and multipart messages; this remains a bounded parser, not a claim to support every possible MIME message.

Historical bounce records restore address suppression. Queues previously switched to an alternate inbox are quarantined. A delivery failure never starts outreach to another person automatically. Temporary delay is recorded separately from a hard failure. A later `no` or unsubscribe reply remains actionable even when the sender and subject repeat or a placement was already marked won.

Initial messages and follow-ups share the daily limit. Transport errors with an uncertain delivery outcome become held records rather than automatic retries. SMTP acceptance does not prove recipient delivery. Only a failure at or after SMTP `DATA` (or of unknown stage) is uncertain. A refusal before `DATA` delivered nothing: the prospect keeps its place, and a rejected Gmail login or sending-limit answer stops sending for the rest of the cycle. A mailbox refused at `RCPT` (5xx) is handled as a bounce: the address is suppressed, no follow-up is sent and nobody else at that publisher is tried automatically.

A conversation's status only moves forward: a later neutral or question reply never turns a positive, won or lost conversation back into "replied", a delivery notice for a later email never changes a replied, positive, won, lost or declined conversation, and an opt-out always becomes declined with domain and address suppression. A reply that starts with no, nope, stop or unsubscribe (greeting and signature aside, e.g. "No" followed by "Sent from my iPhone") is an opt-out, as are "not a fit" / "isn't a fit" / "doesn't fit us", "please remove us", "take us off your list", "opt me out", "don't send further emails", "we'll pass" and an explicit refusal to link (text quoted below an Outlook "From:/Sent:" header is not read as theirs); a "yes" that a negation turns around ("we have never linked") is never read as positive. Replies stored under the earlier, narrower rules are read again once with these rules (again on 2026-11-28 for the closing sentence's answers), and a stored reply still waiting for an answer is read again every cycle before its draft is counted as answered. An "unsubscribe" subject (also after Re:/Fwd:) or our message id in the subject (the List-Unsubscribe mailto, when that header is on) is an opt-out for the matched publisher even when it comes from a different, forwarding address; an opt-out without our ids from a colleague at the same publisher domain, or from someone answering a forwarded copy that quotes the pitched address, is tied to that publisher (declined, domain and addresses suppressed, no follow-up); an opt-out that matches no publisher but answers our outreach (our ids, an unsubscribe subject, a Re:/Fwd: subject, our subject, or a publisher domain we emailed) is counted and its sender suppressed. Answers to replies (`REPLY_MODE`, default `draft`) are saved to Gmail Drafts for the owner, one per reply. Paid-placement offers (fees, prices, costs, rates, "if you pay", a contribution, a number next to a currency or "per year/post/link") get no draft, only the owner flag; guest-post offers, link-exchange requests on a pitch that offered none, and requests for the link back from the pitched site or a site we did not offer get a draft that only asks questions (or the 3-way offer, see "Owner decisions"); no answer agrees to or declines any terms other than confirming the 3-way exchange the owner approved in advance, and an AI-written answer that mentions money or terms is replaced by the template (our page's exact title is the only exception). An AI-written exchange confirmation must carry exactly the three pages (their article, our page, the page on C), the "a separate site I run" disclosure and the "once yours is live" condition, and nothing else promised, or the template is used. Answers carry a List-Unsubscribe header only when that header is turned on. A reply from an address at the domain of a publisher in the contact-form queue is matched to it when nothing else matches. `REPLY_MODE=send` lets an answer to an unconditional positive reply without a question and without any number in it (including the confirmation of a 3-way exchange they accepted that way) go out automatically (a neutral reply only ever gets a draft) only while outreach sending is on, only to the address the conversation started with when the reply came from that same address, with a fresh positive mailbox proof (catch-all and unknown never pass), a current complete reply scan and Sent check, no pending mailbox rebuild, never to a recipient who already received two emails within a day, and at most once per conversation; everything else stays a draft. If Gmail refuses the automatic send before the message is transmitted, automatic sending stops for that cycle and the same answer is saved as a draft. The owner's own answer in Gmail's Sent folder closes the waiting conversation and records the owner's decision.

A new vault (no vault file, or "Create a new vault key" on the dashboard) knows nobody who was emailed or opted out, so it starts held: no pitch, follow-up, automatic answer or pre-verification credit is spent until Gmail's Sent folder (400 days, at least 90 read in full) and All Mail have been read back to restore contacted publishers, bounces and opt-outs. Drafts for the owner are still written. An unfinished All Mail scan continues next cycle from its own cursor; the watchdog raises `mailbox_rebuild_pending` while the hold lasts (an action when the mailbox cannot be read or the hold passes 2 days).

## Production release checklist

- Full repository build, Node tests, Python GSC tests and secret guards must pass.
- Keep the separate workflow safety hold after merging this repair.
- Verify a real provider response for an owner-approved address, without sending outreach.
- Review the existing queued contacts and recorded hard failures through the new policy; keep unknown/catch-all addresses held.
- Verify the mailbox scan is complete and old suppressions are present.
- Only then explicitly approve a small controlled sending release and removal of the workflow hold.

No live messages or paid verification calls were performed to develop these tests. In-memory mailer injection is used by unit tests; the default production Gmail transport independently checks recipient proof before calling SMTP. Direct use of low-level `smtpSend` is not an authorized application sending path.
